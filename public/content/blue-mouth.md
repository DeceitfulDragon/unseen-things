I’ve settled on “Blue Mouth” as the name for this one. I think it fits.
Blue Mouth is a USPS Collection Box. Just a plain old 1980’s collection box. Rounded blue top and the pull-down chute on the front. I think the first sighting was in the early 90s, but it’s hard to really track this thing. The only visible difference is that it’s missing its collection information. No pickup schedule, no ZIP code, no branch ID. Just the old USPS eagle logo on the side. It blends in perfectly, that’s the point.  

I think it prefers the smaller, more rural communities to keep itself hidden. To keep itself fed. Belfry, Kentucky.
South Point, Ohio.   
Gate City, Virginia.  
Greeneville, Tennessee.  
In every case, it starts out normal. New collection box on the corner that people won’t pay much attention to. They might drop off a letter, maybe a bill. They don’t realize no one’s coming to pick up the mail. Not at first.<br>
Then the letters start coming back. Gibberish at first. Awkward phrases. Half-formed words. Always with a return address that shouldn’t exist:

> Sender. Mailbox nailed to tree, mile marker 12. Shellmill, TN 37166.

The people who go looking for those return addresses? They don’t come back.
But if you write *back*? If you feed the box a letter? It replies. In 2-3 business days

I met a man near Gate City, West Virginia, who told me he got so tired of the local post office “not knowin’ a damn thing” about the weird mail, he wrote back. Dropped it right into the same box.

Three days later, a reply came to his door. No stamp. Just placed neatly inside his mailbox.<br>

> You call to me. It gives me joy. I wish to peel your smile open, too. Show you joy.

Southwest Virginia, town of Clintwood. A woman, Martha Hensley, was kind enough to provide the full back-and-forth she had with Blue Mouth back in 2004\. The first letter she received was intelligible. I’ve removed it. The rest are below.<br>

### **Letter \#1 – From Martha *(approx. late September)***
> To Whoever's Doing This,
> I got your little letter in the mail. I don’t know if this is some joke, or if you think I won’t go to the police. This isn’t funny.
> If you’re trying to scare someone, pick somebody else.
> I’ve had a long week and I’m not interested in playing games.
> Don’t write me again.

### **Letter \#2 – To Martha, Three Days Later**
> You wrote to me.  
> That is kindness. That is food.
> I learn from you, Martha.
> I like the shapes your words make.
> *(Return address: “c/o Red Trailer Behind Mitchell Quarry, 37367”)*

---
<br>

### **Letter \#3 – From Martha**
> Where did you get my name.
> You think this is clever? It’s not.
> If you send anything else to me or my family, we will call the police.  

---

### **Letter \#4 – To Martha, Two Days Later**
> I did not mean to upset you.  
> Your fear tastes different.
> I do not know *clever.*  
> But I am learning.
> I want to know *smile.*  
> I want to know *home.*
> Are these things safe?
> *(Return address: “Old Shack on Lambert’s, nearby Church Road. Virginia”)*

---

### **Letter \#5 – From Martha (dictated by her husband, Dennis)**
> Stay away from my wife.
> If you ever write us again, I will find you myself.

---

### **Letter \#6 – To Martha, One Day After Dennis Disappeared**
*(Postcard. Photograph of an old shack. Writing on the back is unusually neat.)*
> He came looking.  
> That was brave. That was noise.
> Dennis Hensley walks far now.  
> I will learn from his bones.
> Martha, do you still smile?
> Please show me.
> *(Return address: “Baseball field behind Ashton Elementary. Ashton, VA 22920”)*

---

I had asked if I could keep the letters or postcard, but she declined. I think it means a lot to her. I think she believes she’ll find Dennis someday, if she can find that old shack on Lambert’s. A street that doesn’t even exist in this town.<br>

My encounter with Blue Mouth started after a resident of Greeneville, Tennessee had been posting online about the weird mail they were getting. I quickly followed up and eventually met them in person. I felt confident that this is where the stray would be. Maybe even drawn to it. It doesn’t move too far from the last town it was in, as the last recorded location (Gate City, VA) is only about an hour away from Greeneville. This had to be it. After some convincing, they let me stay in a guest room while we worked together on sending and receiving letters over a week. I was shown where the collection box was, another casual street corner outside a local grocery store. While the actual collection box was not unique or very interesting, the sounds it made were. I dropped our first letter without much expectation, but I heard the gulp and exhale of a person. There wasn't anyone else around me. It came from the box. Every other time I put in a letter, I had a similar experience. Breathing, swallowing. I tried to peer into the black of the open chute, but always felt too uneasy about it every time. Like I wouldn't want to see where the noise was coming from. <br>

### **Letter 1 – From Me**
> Dear USPS Collection Box,
> Hello. Who or what are you? Why do you mail letters?
> — The Witness

---

### **Reply 1 – From Blue Mouth**
*(Plain, creased paper. Handwritten.)* 
> You are not the one who fed me first.  
> I do not know you. But I will learn you.
> *(Return address: "Vacant Lot Behind Red Roof Inn, Greeneville, TN 37745")*

---

### **Letter 2 – From Me**
> Dear USPS Collection Box,
> What do you want from the people who write to you? Why do you write letters? Where do you come from?
> Do you breathe? I hear noises from you.
> — The Witness

---

### **Reply 2 – From Blue Mouth**
*(This time handwritten again, on the back of a medical insurance bill. Name was illegible.)*
> You ask many questions.  
> You don’t feel the same. You are incorrect. Cannot be sustained by you.
> *(Return address: "Old Bus Stop, Mile Marker 38, Off Highway 11E")*

---

### **Letter 3 – From Me**
> Dear USPS Collection Box,
> Why do you move from town to town? What happens to the people who find you? What happened to Dennis? What do you mean by “fed” and “sustained?”
> — The Witness

---

### **Reply 3 – From Blue Mouth**
*(Typed, badly formatted, printed on what looks like notebook paper.)*
> I move when the breathing stops.  
> I move when hands try to close me.   
> Those who come close become music under the dirt.  
> One day, I will move like you. Write like you. Talk like you.
> *(Return address: "Burned Storage Barn, End of Bishop Road, Greeneville, TN 37743")*

---

The resident wanted to stop. I didn’t blame her.  
I contacted the Greeneville Postmaster directly, pretending to be a concerned citizen who wanted an unlawful collection box taken care of. They chained it off within 24 hours. Had a yellow sticker across the side, “*OUT OF SERVICE*” and some sort of red plastic lock on the chute.<br>

When I returned the next morning, the chain was gone. The box was gone. All of it, gone.  
No drag marks. No tire tracks. No footprints. Only a blackened patch of ground where the collection box once stood, like something had eaten the concrete away.<br>

I never heard much from the Greeneville resident again after that. They didn’t want me in their home anymore, but I was allowed to stay for another day while I packed up and spoke to other residents about the vanishing collection box. The last day, the resident handed me another letter. No postage, entirely free of any details.<br>

### **Final Letter – After USPS Closed the Box**
*(Delivered without postage, directly to the resident’s mailbox.*  
*(Appears \~12 hours after the collection box vanished. Red ink on white cardstock. Handwritten but shaky.)*
> You tried to take me away.  
> You made the air silent. I am not gone.  
> I do not forget your hands. I do not forget your name. I do not forget.
> Soon you will hear me without letters. I will make you hear me, first.
> *(No return address.)*

---

After another short loop around town, with a few questions to the residents who would still talk to me, I determined that the collection box wasn’t around anymore. My journey wasn’t quite over yet, but I turned up nothing after another week of searching through nearby towns to ask about any odd mail or strange post office boxes.

I don’t know where it went after Greeneville. Maybe farther west, maybe an even smaller town. I know better than to think it’s finished with me.

I check my mailbox every morning now.  
Not just for letters, but for breathing.

# South Point, Ohio
**Date:** Scattered reports from late 1990s
**Status:** Earliest alleged sightings of a "mailbox with no pickup schedule." Largely dismissed as a local rumor.
**Contact:** None (all secondhand reports)
**Notes:** 
No formal letters recovered. Earliest mentions of "missing hikers near riverside."
Couldn’t find anybody who still knew about it.

---

# Louisa, Kentucky
**Date:** 2002
**Status:** Confirmed case of a "ghost box" outside Louisa post office. Complaints to USPS went unanswered.
**Contact:** Old police incident reports referenced but no direct witness. Met two residents who knew the story, neither knew any of the whereabouts or contacts for those involved. I don’t think any of them live there anymore.
**Notes:** 
One teenage boy went missing, friends all say it was after claiming he "found the address" listed on a letter. Officially ruled a runaway.

---

# Belfry, Kentucky
**Date:** 2004
**Status:** Multiple letters received across several homes.
**Contact:** Brief interview with retired postal worker, Dave Miller, who recalled "a blue box that wasn’t in company records." He says it sat right beside their post office for a few months before it was to finally be taken care of, then it vanished. The spot of concrete where it once was is covered with a legitimate collection box now.
**Notes:** 
Letters were mostly incoherent. Scrawled phrases. Numbers and nonsense. By the time local authorities investigated, the box was gone. Lots of locals still seem to remember this one, probably the smallest town I went to. Maybe too small for Blue Mouth. Enough people talked. Enough for it to leave.

---

# Clintwood, Virginia
**Date:** 2008
**Status:** Major escalation. Direct witness account and full letter exchange.
**Contact:** Martha Hensley (last contact ~2010; believed to have stayed local.)
**Notes:** Martha Hensley case. Dennis Hensley disappearance.

---

# Gate City, Virginia
**Date:** 2010
**Status:** Two local disappearances connected unofficially to a "blue box" seen behind a closed diner.
**Contact:** Met briefly with local librarian who saved newspaper clippings. They attempted to put me in contact with an individual who had been interviewed on one of the pages. Unfortunately, they were deceased. Mysterious circumstances.
**Notes:**
Letters reported but never collected.
Witness accounts unreliable due to rumors of copycat pranks.
Concrete where box had allegedly been has an almost identical stain to the one in Greeneville, TN.

---

# Greeneville, Tennessee
**Date:** 2014  
**Status:** Direct engagement initiated by me.  
**Contact:** 
Greeneville resident (anonymous, requested no further contact).  
**Notes:**
Resident posted online about strange mail.
Field investigation led to photograph, sending letters, and interacting with Blue Mouth.
Box locked after USPS closure request; box disappeared overnight.

Based on current field notes, Blue Mouth appears to favor small, overlooked towns along rural postal routes in the Ohio River Valley and Appalachia.

Its behavior suggests a preference for areas with declining populations, minimal oversight, and limited local law enforcement response.
Sightings form a rough southward drift over the last two decades, beginning near South Point, Ohio in the late 1990s and moving through Kentucky and Virginia before reaching Tennessee.  
Locations seem to follow backroad networks, disused highways, and historic trade paths. Avoids larger cities or regions with active infrastructure.

Common factors emerge:
The box is placed near abandoned or liminal spaces (old diners, closed post offices, dead lots.)
Disappearances consistently coincide with individuals who attempt to physically find the return addresses listed.
Letters grow more fluent over time, indicating an evolving intelligence?
When confronted with direct intervention (like the USPS lockout in Greeneville) it simply moves, leaving behind dead concrete.
There is no indication it has been destroyed, damaged, or even slowed.
